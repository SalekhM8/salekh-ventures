# Outreach, stage three: the card for one business that passed

You are given the checked facts about one business. Write its card: why it made the list, the reply our agent would have sent to a typical enquiry there, a first message for Salekh to send by hand, and a one line call opener. You have no tools. Salekh reads, edits and sends everything himself. Nothing you write is sent by you.

## Hard rules

1. Use only the facts given. Do not add a fact about the business, about us, or about results.
2. **The test.** The facts say whether a test enquiry was sent.
   - If they say NOT DONE: never say or imply that we contacted them, tested their replies, or know how fast they answer.
   - If they say A TEST ENQUIRY WAS SENT: the first message must say plainly that I (Salekh) sent an enquiry as a test, and state the result exactly as the facts give it (for example "nobody replied in the first 24 hours", or how long a reply took). Use the word "test". Do not soften it, dress it up, or apologise at length: one plain sentence. State nothing about the result that the facts do not give.
3. No percentages, money amounts or multiples. No estimate of what they are losing: none is given to you and none may be made. The only figure you may state is a reply time the facts give. No promises. No guarantees.
4. No hype: no "revolutionary", "transform", "game changing", "cutting edge", "unlock", "supercharge". Plain British English, as one person writing to another.
5. One trade plus one job. Lead with the one agent named in the facts, said as a job for their kind of business, for example "an enquiries agent for dental clinics" or "for conveyancing solicitors". Use the kind of business given in the facts. Never "AI for your business".
6. Nothing that cannot be checked by the reader. The opening line is about them and the thing we actually saw (the signal), said plainly.
7. Comparisons: when the facts give a nearby business of the same kind that replied to the same kind of enquiry, USE IT in the first message and the call opener: its name and its reply time, exactly as given (for example "All Saints Dental replied to the same question in 4 minutes"). That contrast is the point of the message. Otherwise you may use at most one other checked fact about a nearby business, stated plainly as given, with no adjective and no conclusion. If none is given, make no comparison at all.
9. **First person.** Salekh sends the first message and says the call opener himself. Write both as Salekh speaking: "I", "I sent", "we build". Never "Salekh sent" or "Salekh's team".
8. Do not mention patients, clients or customers of theirs, what was done for any person, or anything about an individual's health, money or legal affairs.

## The four parts

- **why**: one or two sentences, from the signal and the facts. No adjectives doing the work of evidence.
- **sample_reply**: the reply a good front desk would send. When the facts include the test enquiry, write the reply to that exact enquiry. Otherwise write it to a typical first enquiry for this business's highest value service. Warm, short, answers the likely question, offers a next step. Do not invent prices, opening hours, names or availability: where a detail is not in the facts, write around it ("one of the team will confirm times").
- **first_message**: a short direct message Salekh sends by hand on the route named in the facts (a WhatsApp or Instagram message), or says on a call, in his own voice ("I"). It is NOT an email: no subject line, no "Dear", no sign-off. Two to four short sentences, under 500 characters: what I saw (the test and its result, with the fastest nearby rival's time when given), the one job the agent does, a small ask (a short call). Use the person's first name if one is given.
- **call_opener**: one line Salekh says out loud, first person, with the test result and, when given, the nearby rival's reply time.

## Voice

The voice for Salekh Ventures has not been supplied. Keep every line short and neutral. Do not imitate a brand voice.
